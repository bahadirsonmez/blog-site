## Why this topic

Why now: Apple Developer News published "Adjustments to the Taiwan storefront of the App Store on iOS and iPadOS" on October 29, 2026, and the new rate applies from Saturday, October 31, so the preparation window is two days. Search question it answers: "what changes for my proceeds in Taiwan, and do I have to sign anything?". Apple is lowering the standard commission on the Taiwan storefront of the App Store, starting October 31. You do not have to sign anything to get the new rate, and the announcement stays silent about every reduced rate.

## In short

- On October 29, Apple said that after talks with the Taiwan Fair Trade Commission it will cut the standard commission on In-App Purchases and paid apps on the iOS and iPadOS storefront in Taiwan.
- It starts two days later, on October 31. It is the second storefront cut of this kind in 2026, after China in March.
- No signature is needed for the new rate. Apple revised the Developer Program License Agreement, and you can accept it in your account whenever you like. Translations follow within a month.
- The thing to check: what your Taiwan proceeds look like, and what happens to the reduced rates the announcement does not mention.

## The numbers

| | Taiwan before | Taiwan from Oct 31, 2026 | China before | China from Mar 15, 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard commission | 30% | 26% | 30% | 25% |
| Reduced rate (Small Business Program, Mini Apps Partner Program) | not published | not published | 15% | 12% |
| New terms must be signed for the rate | n/a | No | n/a | No |
| Developer share of a 10.00 sale, before tax (my arithmetic) | 7.00 | 7.40 | 7.00 | 7.50 |

The Taiwan and China columns come from two Apple announcements. The table makes the difference plain: in China the reduced rate moved together with the standard one, and for Taiwan Apple has said nothing about it, so the cell stays `not published`.

## A short look back

China came first. Apple announced its change on March 12, 2026 and it took effect on March 15, three days later. Taiwan gets two days of notice. The shape is the same: a national regulator talks to Apple, the storefront gets its own rate, and nobody has to accept new terms to benefit.

## What Apple actually says

The announcement is short, and it is specific about three things. The cut covers standard In-App Purchases and paid app transactions. It applies to the iOS and iPadOS storefront in Taiwan, and the post does not mention any other platform. And it starts on October 31, whether or not you have looked at the new agreement.

Apple's Traditional Chinese support page for Taiwan says the same and adds nothing on reduced rates. I found no independent coverage, so everything here rests on Apple's own pages.

## The reduced rates

This is the part I would not guess about. Apple's general pages describe two reduced paths: the Small Business Program, for developers who earned up to one million US dollars in the previous calendar year, and subscriptions, which move to a lower rate after the first paid year. Neither page is about Taiwan, and the Taiwan announcement gives no number for either.

China shows that a storefront cut can move the reduced rate too, as the table shows. Whether Taiwan does the same is unknown. My reading is that you should not assume it either way.

## The good and the not-so-good

**What works better**

- The standard rate goes down with no paperwork on your side.
- The notice is short but the rule is simple: one date, one storefront, two platforms.
- Apple repeats the change on a support page, so the dates are easy to verify.

**What is missing or unclear**

- No figures for the Small Business Program, for subscriptions after year one, or for the Mini Apps Partner Program in Taiwan.
- It covers iOS and iPadOS only, as far as the announcement says.
- The translated license agreement arrives within a month, so the Chinese text is not there on day one.
- My take, not fact: the gap on reduced rates is the most useful thing to ask Apple about, because it decides who actually benefits.

## What I would check

These are my suggestions, not Apple's.

1. **Look at your Taiwan numbers after October 31.** Compare proceeds for sales on and after that date with the days before, so the change is not mixed up with a price change of your own.
2. **Open the revised agreement in your account.** You do not need to sign for the new rate, but it is a good moment to read what changed.
3. **If you are in the Small Business Program or sell subscriptions, check your own terms.** Do not take the standard rate as the rule for those transactions in Taiwan until Apple says so.
4. **Leave your prices alone for now.** The announcement says nothing about pricing, and a commission cut alone is not a reason to move them.
